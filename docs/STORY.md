# Aeterna: the story

Status: APPROVED by the user 2026-11-10 (ending = a choice at the shrine; the Warden is named Skotos). Parts are being built (cards, hands, Veil, loop rules, thoughts data are in the game). Gameplay numbers stay in `docs/GAME_SPEC.md`; this file is story, cast and words.

> 2026-11-11 (the user): the shrine is now a **statue** of the little god itself: hooded, hands cupped round a small ember, on a plinth, bigger than a thumb. "The shrine" in this file means that statue; the villagers still pray before it.

Tone: warm, plain, a little funny, then quietly heartbreaking. Cozy, never grim. The sadness comes from love, not from gore. Short sentences. Villagers sound like neighbours, not prophets.

## 1. The premise in one breath
You are a very small god. Your whole world is one floating island, a meadow, twelve people, a well and a shrine. A forgotten god, the Warden, has cursed your protective Veil so it fails every night. Each time the village falls, you turn back time. The player is never told that night 5 always falls. They find out the way the villagers do: slowly, by remembering.

## 2. You
- A god of a few hundred years' patience and one small power: the **Veil**, a dome of warm light woven from the villagers' **Faith**.
- You cannot speak. The villagers speak to you, at the shrine, every dawn. You answer with what you can do: place defences, lift a fallen kite, light a lantern.
- You love them the way you love a garden. Possessively, tenderly, and without ever being thanked to your face.
- The player is you. The hands are yours: ember-gold, a little too bright, as if a god tried to be small.

## 3. The Warden
- Once the god of the Dark Wood that wraps the island. Their people stopped praying; their temple rotted into the roots. A forgotten god starves.
- They are not a monster in their own mind. They say they want the villagers' faith "because it is the only warm thing left." The deeper truth: they want you to know what being forgotten feels like.
- Voice: cool, amused, tired, sometimes almost kind. Never shouts. Purple is their colour (art rule).
- Curse, spoken at the first dusk: *"Your Veil will fail at every nightfall. Let us see how long they believe in a god who cannot keep the dark out."*
- Their true name is **Skotos** (Greek for darkness; the villagers only ever call them the Warden). It is carved on the shrine's oldest stone and can only be read through the Crystal Lens (see section 9).

## 5. Loop 1: what exactly happens (scene by scene)
Loop 1 is the player's first time and the villagers' first time. Nobody knows anything.

1. **Title.** The real room, softened and warm. On the table a small island sleeps under fireflies. A carved plaque: *Wake*.
2. **Waking.** Your hands glow into being. Maren, kneeling at the shrine: *"Little god, if you're listening… the elders say the Veil is wearing thin. Please. Keep us."* Five lines of narration on parchment: you are the god of this island; your Veil keeps the dark away; it is woven from their faith; tonight something is coming; watch over them.
3. **Day 1, morning.** A wooden tray rises with three cards. Pip, pointing at the tree line: *"The woods were loud last night."* The player lifts the first card, sets a tower on the grass, and the villagers cheer. Faith rises.
4. **Day 1, midday.** Wren's kite is caught in the old oak. The player lifts it down. Wren runs to the shrine with a honeycake. Gold motes rise from her into the Veil. The player taps a villager and hears their thoughts for the first time.
5. **Dusk.** The sky goes amber. A purple crack runs across the Veil. The Warden: *"Nightfall, little god. Your light goes out."* The Veil falls as embers. Horns in the Dark Wood.
6. **Night 1.** Easy. The towers hold. Dawn: the Veil knits itself back, Maren lights a candle, Faith rises. The first reward beat.
7. **Days 2 to 4.** Waves grow, cards arrive, small lives unfold: Tomas's cradle, Edda and the miller, Maren teaching Pip the prayers.
8. **Night 5.** The Warden comes in person. The player cannot win. The houses go dark one by one. The last image: Maren at the shrine, still praying.
9. **The rewind.** The hourglass rises from the shrine. The world runs backwards: embers return to the sky, windows re-light, villagers walk backwards to their beds. Day 1, morning. Maren is kneeling. She says the same words.

## 6. The arc across five loops
The villagers do not know they are looping, but love leaves a mark. A little more survives each time.

| Loop | The player feels | The world shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I can win this." | Night 5 plays as a very hard night. |
| 2 | "I'll do better." | Same dawn prayer, but Sorrel frowns: *"I dreamed today already."* A hairline crack on the shrine. The Warden's horn sounds at the same moment on night 5. |
| 3 | "Wait…" | Pip: *"Last time the mill burned. Didn't it?"* The Warden speaks to you: *"Again? You know how this ends."* The crack spreads. Night 5 falls at the same moment no matter how strong you are. |
| 4 | "It can't be won." | The villagers remember openly. Maren: *"We've lived this day before, haven't we."* Some despair (Faith dips). Some grow kinder. Tomas finishes the cradle early "in case there's no time." Thoughts turn into goodbyes. |
| 5 | The truth. | Maren, at dawn: *"You've been carrying us. Let us carry you."* |

Rules for writing clues: never say "loop", "time" or "rewind" in a villager's mouth before loop 4. Say *dream*, *déjà vu*, *haven't we done this*. The player should be the one who says the word.

## 7. The truth
The curse cannot be beaten by force, because it feeds on your refusal to let go. Each rewind tightens it. The Warden does not want to kill the village; they want to be remembered, and you are the only one who can give them that. You keep winning the small fight and losing the real one.

## 8. The endings (the user picks one or keeps both as a choice)
**A. Let go (bittersweet).** On the last night you do not rewind. You spend your godhood to make the Veil permanent and fade into a small carved statue on the shrine. The last shot is the morning after: villagers leave honeycakes at your feet. Wren whispers *"Thank you, little god."* The Warden, watching from the trees, finally weeps. Credits.

**B. Remember them (hopeful).** You show the Warden the villagers' prayers, every one you have heard across the loops. The Warden remembers their own people, and the curse cracks. The Dark Wood blooms. You both keep watch, two small gods on two edges of one garden.

## 9. Hidden lore (read through the Crystal Lens)

1. *"Here the first god kept a small fire, so that no one would walk home in the dark."* (the original purpose of the shrine)
2. *"Her name was ____."* (the Warden's name, worn away; fills in a letter per loop)
3. *"The Wood was a garden once."* (the Warden's story)
4. From loop 3: golden threads between villagers who remember, visible only through the lens.

## 10. Dawn prayers (Maren, every morning)
One line a day, changing by loop. Loop 1 samples (days 1 to 5):
1. *"Little god, if you're listening… the Veil is thin. Please keep us."*
2. *"Thank you for yesterday. The baby kicked last night. Isolde says it is a good omen."*
3. *"I told Pip the old prayer. He asked if you get bored. I said gods don't get bored. I hope that's true."*
4. *"The woods are louder every night. I'm not afraid. I want you to know that."*
5. *"Whatever comes tonight, thank you for the mornings."*

Loop 2 changes: she says the same first line, then pauses: *"…Funny. I've said that before."* Loop 4: *"Good morning, little god. I think you know me better than I know myself. Let's try again."* Loop 5: see section 6.
